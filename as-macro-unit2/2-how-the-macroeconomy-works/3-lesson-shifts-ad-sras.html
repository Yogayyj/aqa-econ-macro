Lesson 3: AD, SRAS, LRAS, Equilibrium and Shocks

Spec focus (3.2.2.2): factors shifting AD/SRAS, LRAS, equilibrium, and macro shocks in AD-AS analysis.

Mind Map Overview

AD-AS analysis links demand shifts, supply shifts and LRAS to changes in equilibrium output, price level and macroeconomic performance.
AD Shifts

Right shift

Higher C, I, G or X and/or lower M increase real output and price level.

Left shift

Lower spending reduces real output and creates downward pressure on prices.

Demand-side shocks

Unexpected changes in spending conditions move AD quickly.

SRAS Shifts

Cost rises

Higher wages, energy or raw material prices shift SRAS left.

Cost falls

Lower costs or improved productivity shift SRAS right.

Supply-side shocks

Unexpected cost or productivity changes affect inflation and output.

LRAS and Capacity

LRAS

Shows the economy's productive potential at full employment output.

Shift right

More/better factors of production raise productive capacity.

Policy link

Supply-side policies aim to increase LRAS over time.

Equilibrium Effects

AD = AS

Equilibrium sets real national output and the price level.

Positive outcome

Rising output can reduce unemployment but may increase inflation.

Negative outcome

Falling output can increase unemployment and slow growth.

1) AD Curve Shifts

An AD shift happens when non-price-level factors change.

  • Consumer confidence
  • Business confidence and investment
  • Taxes on income
  • Government spending
  • World growth and exports

Right shift = more demand at every price level. Left shift = less demand at every price level.

Rightward shift of AD

2) SRAS Curve Shifts

SRAS shifts mainly because of changes in costs of production.

  • Raw material and energy prices
  • Wages
  • Indirect taxes and subsidies
  • Productivity
Rule: Rising costs shift SRAS left (less output at any price level). Falling costs shift SRAS right.
Leftward shift of SRAS (cost rise)

3) LRAS and Underlying Growth

LRAS shows normal capacity output (the sustainable long-run level of real output).

A rightward LRAS shift represents underlying growth in productive capacity.
  • More factors of production (labour, capital, resources)
  • Higher productivity (technology, efficiency, skills)
Rightward shift of LRAS (underlying growth)

4) Macroeconomic Equilibrium and Shocks

Equilibrium occurs where AD = AS, determining the equilibrium price level and real national income.

Above equilibrium price level: excess supply and downward pressure on prices. Below equilibrium: excess demand and upward pressure on prices.

AD-AS equilibrium (point E is exactly on both curves)
  • Positive demand shock: AD right; usually raises output and price level.
  • Negative demand shock: AD left; usually lowers output and price level.
  • Positive supply shock: SRAS right; raises output and lowers price level.
  • Negative supply shock: SRAS left; lowers output and raises price level.

7) common questions: Long run and short run

These two statements do not conflict because they describe two different “beyond-capacity” situations.

Situation A: Cannot go beyond current maximum capacity (sustainable limit)

When the economy is already at full capacity (long-run potential output), real output cannot keep increasing in the short run without changing productive capacity. Long-run growth requires LRAS shifting right (better productivity, more capital, more resources, better technology).

Situation B: Can temporarily produce above normal capacity (unsustainable boom)

In the short run, output can exceed normal/trend capacity through overtime, labour shortages, and intensive machine use. But this creates cost pressure, stronger inflation, and wear-and-tear, so it cannot be sustained.

One-line summary for students: Sustainable long-run growth needs LRAS to shift right; temporary over-capacity output is possible in the short run but is inflationary and unsustainable.
