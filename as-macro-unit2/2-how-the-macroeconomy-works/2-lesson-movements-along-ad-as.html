Lesson 2: Movements Along the AD and AS Curves

Spec focus (3.2.2.2): why changes in the price level are represented by movements along AD and AS curves.

Before the Curves: What AD and AS Mean

Aggregate demand (AD) is total planned spending on a country’s output at different price levels.

AD components: C + I + G + (X − M)
  • C (Consumption): household spending on goods and services.
  • I (Investment): firms’ spending on capital (machines, factories, software, inventories).
  • G (Government spending): current spending by government on goods and services.
  • X − M (Net exports): exports add to domestic demand; imports are spending on foreign output, so they are subtracted.

Why these components? Together, they capture all major spending flows on domestically produced output in the macroeconomy.

Aggregate supply (AS) is total output that firms are willing and able to produce at different price levels.

  • In the short run, firms respond to profitability and production costs.
  • In the long run, supply depends on productive capacity: labour, capital, resources, and productivity.
Exam tip: AD is about planned spending; AS is about planned output.

Movements Along the AD Curve

The aggregate demand (AD) curve shows the inverse relationship between the price level and real national income demanded.

Key idea: A fall in the price level causes a movement along AD, increasing aggregate demand (ceteris paribus).
  • Competitiveness effect: lower domestic prices can increase exports and reduce imports.
  • Real wealth effect: lower prices raise the real value of money balances and fixed-value assets, supporting spending.

So, movement from a higher price level (PL1) to a lower one (PL2) means output demanded rises from Y1 to Y2.

Movements Along the AS Curve

The aggregate supply (AS) curve shows the direct relationship between the price level and real output supplied.

Key idea: A change in the price level causes a movement along AS, not a shift.

If price rises, production tends to be more profitable, so firms increase output. If price falls, firms reduce output.

Exam tip: “Movement along” means only the price level changed. All other determinants are held constant.
