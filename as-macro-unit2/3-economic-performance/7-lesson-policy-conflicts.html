Lesson 7: Possible Conflicts between Macroeconomic Policy Objectives

Use AD/AS and careful time-frame analysis to explain trade-offs—and how policy can sometimes reconcile them.

Learning objectives

  • Analyse how growth affects the environment, income distribution, unemployment and the budget balance.
  • Connect negative and positive output gaps to cyclical unemployment and inflationary pressure.
  • Use AD/AS to distinguish short-run conflicts from long-run possibilities.
  • Evaluate demand-management and supply-side policies that may reconcile objectives.

1. Why objectives may conflict

Objective pursuedPossible benefitPossible conflict
Faster economic growthHigher living standards, jobs and tax revenue.Inflation near capacity; pollution and resource depletion; gains may be unequal.
Lower inflationGreater price stability and competitiveness.Deflationary demand policy may slow growth and raise cyclical unemployment.
Lower unemploymentHigher output, income and inclusion.Rapid AD growth may create inflation once spare capacity is exhausted.
Improved budget balanceSlower debt accumulation and more fiscal space.Tax rises or spending cuts may reduce AD, growth and employment in the short run.

2. Output gaps organise the analysis

Negative output gap

Actual output is below potential. Cyclical unemployment is high and demand-pull inflationary pressure is weak. Expansionary demand policy can raise output and employment with a relatively small initial price-level effect.

Positive output gap

Actual output exceeds sustainable capacity. Labour and other resources become scarce, creating wage and price pressure. Further AD growth mainly raises the price level and is unlikely to sustain real growth.

AD rises with spare capacity→real output and jobs rise strongly→gap closes→inflation pressure increases

3. Growth and wider objectives

Environment

More production can increase emissions, congestion and resource use. Yet higher incomes and tax receipts can fund cleaner technology, and regulation or carbon pricing can reduce environmental costs.

Income distribution

Job creation may reduce poverty, but gains can accrue mainly to owners of capital or skilled workers. The effect depends on the pattern of growth and tax-and-benefit policy.

Unemployment

Short-run growth from higher AD reduces cyclical unemployment. Long-run growth from productivity and LRAS can create jobs, although labour-saving change may cause transitional structural unemployment.

Budget balance

Growth raises tax receipts and reduces some welfare spending, improving the cyclical budget balance. Growth created by deficit-financed stimulus may initially increase borrowing.

4. Reconciling objectives

Policy approachShort runLong run / evaluation
Expansionary fiscal or monetary policyCan close a negative output gap and lower cyclical unemployment.Risks inflation, imports and debt if mistimed; effectiveness depends on multiplier, confidence and spare capacity.
Contractionary demand policyReduces demand-pull inflation and may improve imports or the budget.Can cause a negative output gap and unemployment; credibility may lower inflation expectations.
Supply-side policyTraining and infrastructure may cost money and work slowly.Shifts LRAS right, allowing higher output and employment with less inflation; targeting and implementation determine success.
Green growth packageCarbon pricing may raise costs and be regressive without compensation.Investment, innovation and targeted transfers can combine capacity growth, lower emissions and fairness.
Core judgement: a conflict is not fixed. Its scale depends on the output gap, cause of inflation or unemployment, policy design, supply responsiveness and time period.

5. AD/AS exam method

  1. State the starting output gap and objective.
  2. Identify whether AD, SRAS or LRAS shifts, and why.
  3. Explain changes in real output, price level and employment.
  4. Develop the conflict with another objective.
  5. Evaluate using spare capacity, magnitude, time lags and a possible complementary policy.
25-mark planning task: “Policies that increase economic growth must conflict with price stability and environmental objectives.” Build one short-run AD argument, one long-run LRAS argument, and a justified conclusion.

Check your understanding

  1. Why is the inflation–unemployment conflict likely to be smaller in a deep recession?
  2. Explain two effects of growth on the government budget balance.
  3. How could supply-side policy reconcile growth and price stability?
  4. Why might green growth have short-run distributional costs?
  5. Give two reasons why the same policy can have different outcomes in the short and long run.
