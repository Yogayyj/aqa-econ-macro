Lesson 1: Monetary Policy

How a central bank uses interest rates, money, credit and the exchange rate to influence inflation and economic activity.

Mind Map Overview

The central bank influences inflation and activity through interest rates, money, credit and the exchange rate.

Framework

  • Price stability usually primary
  • Targets guide decisions
  • Rates, credit and QE are instruments

Interest rates

  • Consumption and saving
  • Investment and asset prices
  • AD changes after time lags

Exchange rate

  • Net exports alter AD
  • Imported inflation
  • Growth, jobs and current account

QE

  • Large-scale government bond purchases
  • Money supply rises and yields fall
  • Lending and spending may rise

Learning objectives

  • Define monetary policy and distinguish its objective, target and instruments.
  • Explain the transmission of an interest-rate change to consumption, investment, net exports, AD and inflation.
  • Analyse how exchange-rate changes affect each macroeconomic objective.
  • Explain quantitative easing (QE) and evaluate when it may be effective.

1. Objectives, targets and instruments

Monetary policy is action by the central bank to influence interest rates, the supply of money and credit, and the exchange rate.
TermMeaningExample
ObjectiveThe ultimate economic outcome.Price stability is normally primary; stable activity and employment may be subsidiary objectives.
TargetA measurable benchmark used to judge policy.A stated inflation rate or range.
InstrumentThe variable directly changed by the central bank.Policy interest rate, asset purchases or credit measures.
Syllabus boundary: you do not need to explain how interest rates or exchange rates are determined in this unit. You must explain how a change in either affects macroeconomic performance.

2. Interest-rate transmission

policy rate rises→borrowing dearer and saving more attractive→C and I fall→AD falls→inflationary pressure falls

Consumption

Higher rates raise variable debt repayments and the reward for saving. Disposable income and spending may fall. Savers receiving more interest create a possible offset.

Investment

A higher cost of borrowing makes fewer investment projects profitable. Expectations matter: confident firms may still invest.

Asset prices and wealth

Higher rates can weaken property and bond prices, reducing wealth and collateral and therefore spending.

Time lags

Fixed-rate contracts, delayed investment decisions and uncertain confidence mean the full effect can take considerable time.

An interest-rate cut reverses these channels and can close a negative output gap. Its effect may be weak if households repay debt, banks restrict lending or firms remain pessimistic.

3. The exchange-rate channel

ChangeAggregate demandPolicy-objective effects
AppreciationExports become less price competitive and imports cheaper, so net exports and AD tend to fall.Lower imported inflation, but weaker growth and employment; the current account may worsen.
DepreciationExports become more competitive and imports dearer, so net exports and AD tend to rise.Growth and jobs may rise, but imported and demand-pull inflation increase; the current account may improve.

The outcome depends on price elasticities, spare capacity, imported input costs, business pricing decisions and time lags. An improvement in export competitiveness does not guarantee higher export revenue.

4. Quantitative easing and other instruments

Quantitative easing: the central bank creates central-bank money to make large-scale purchases of government bonds, usually from financial institutions.
central bank buys bonds→money supply and bond demand rise→bond yields / longer-term rates fall→credit, asset prices and spending may rise→AD rises

QE may be used when policy interest rates are already very low. Other instruments include guidance about future rates, reserve or liquidity rules, and targeted measures that encourage bank lending.

Evaluation: QE does not guarantee lending or spending. Banks may retain liquidity, confidence may remain low, asset-price gains may increase inequality, and excessive stimulus can cause inflation or currency depreciation.

5. Exam application

Data-response task: Inflation is 7%, output is stagnant and the rise originated in imported energy prices. Analyse whether a rise in interest rates is appropriate. Separate the effect on AD from the original SRAS shock and give a justified judgement.
  1. Explain two channels through which higher interest rates reduce AD.
  2. Why may a rate cut fail to increase investment?
  3. Explain how depreciation affects both inflation and the current account.
  4. How does QE aim to lower longer-term interest rates?
  5. Why may monetary policy face an inflation–unemployment conflict?
